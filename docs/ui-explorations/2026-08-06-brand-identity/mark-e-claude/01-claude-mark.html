Mark E — “The Slot”

Designed by Claude after reviewing directions A–D. Neutral presentation, same conditions as the comparison sheet.

The sentence, drawn: nothing leaves the machine but the answer. A sealed mass — your files, your machine — with one slot cut through its wall, and a single line of text standing outside it. Not a lens (searching is the commodity), not an arrow (data doesn’t stream out), not a keyhole (nobody’s locked out — it’s your room). At display sizes the answer-line carries a footnote marker: what leaves, leaves cited.

Honest self-critique (same test I gave A–D)

What it says without being explained: a solid thing with an opening, and something small that came out of it. The full reading (“only the answer leaves, and it leaves cited”) needs one sentence — the same debt B and D carry.

Accidental readings audited: mail slot (harmless — arguably helpful: things pass through a door that never opens). Battery-with-terminal (the real risk at 16 px; mitigated by keeping the dash detached and the slot cut visibly deep). Logout-door (avoided by using a text-dash, not an arrow). It is not a letterform in any weight I could produce.

What it deliberately refuses: the magnifying glass (commodity), any glyph-in-rounded-app-square formula (B’s genericness), decorative detail that dies below 32 px (C’s oxblood problem).
