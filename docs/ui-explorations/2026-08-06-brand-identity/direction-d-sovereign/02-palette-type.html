02.1 — Light / dark strategy, in one sentence

Dark is not a theme here, it is the ground of the territory: anything that represents your machine — the app, the mark, a parcel diagram — keeps the dark ground in every context, and light is only ever the surround it is placed on (paper, a doc site, a light OS), which is why the mark never inverts.

Practically: one product surface (dark), one document surface (paper), and a single rule for the boundary between them. There is no light build of the app to maintain and no inverted logo file to get wrong.

No colour in this palette exists to signal "AI". Brass is the weathered survey monument, oxide is the boundary ink on a cadastral sheet, patina is what brass becomes after decades in the ground — the same hue family as the shipped teal, taken off the screen and into a material. There is no gradient anywhere in the system.

Oxide carries the boundary because a boundary is a graphic, and 3.7 : 1 clears the 3 : 1 AA threshold for non-text. It is banned from text at every size — the one hard rule in the colour system, and the reason edge labels on the boundary are set in brass or muted, never in the line's own colour at small sizes.

10 / mono / +.16emEdge labels and status

Six steps, no half-steps, no fluid clamps. A survey sheet has a fixed set of lettering heights and so does this: if a piece of text does not fit one of them, it is the wrong piece of text.
