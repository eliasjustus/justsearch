The mark and the wordmark

A bracket, thickened into a binding. The whole identity is built from the apparatus of a cited document — enclosure, margin, footnote rule — because that apparatus is literally what the product produces.

The mark “The Kept Record”. Drawn on a 64-unit grid; strokes on 8-unit multiples.

Containment, not seeking. A magnifying glass draws the commodity half of the product. This draws the differentiator: a bracket — the punctuation of citation — thickened into a binding on the left and a shelf along the foot, with the lines of a record nested inside it. The lines never cross the binding. The last line is set in oxblood: the cited one. The corner stays open on two sides on purpose — a kept record is still being added to.

The wordmark Set solid, one word, no case break, no colour split.

  1. 1just, adv. — merely; the modest claim. just, adv. — simply; the easy one. just, adj. — of justice; the one the record-keepers hear. All three are true at once, which is why the name is never split, tinted, or re-cased to favour one of them.

The wordmark carries a footnote. That is the whole direction in one gesture: a claim, and immediately beneath it, its receipt. The device recurs everywhere — in the hero, in answers, in the result list — and it is never decorative, because the thing being footnoted is always a real source.
